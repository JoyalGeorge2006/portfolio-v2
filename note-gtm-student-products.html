How I think about GTM for student products

Students don't need big launches. They need one narrow use case, distributed where they already hang out, with a clear reason to invite the next person.

GTMStudent productsDistribution

1. Start with one job, one campus

Pick a single painful job — e.g. "understand recursion before tomorrow's lab" — and solve it for one group you can reach in person. If it doesn't spread in one classroom or Discord server, it won't spread across colleges.

2. Distribute where attention already is

  • WhatsApp / Telegram study groups, not ads.
  • Short demo videos (30-60s) showing before → after.
  • Classroom champions: 2-3 friends who onboard others.
Rule I use: if I can't explain who it's for, where they'll find it, and why they'd share it in one sentence each, the GTM isn't ready.

3. Make activation the metric

Track: visited → tried core action → returned in 7 days. For student tools, Day-7 return matters more than signups. I keep a simple sheet: source, first action, repeat.

4. My checklist before sharing

  • One landing line + one demo link
  • Feedback form (3 questions max)
  • Follow-up message for day 2
